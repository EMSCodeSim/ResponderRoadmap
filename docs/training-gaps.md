# Training gaps

## Product definition

A **training gap** is evidence that a member or group is not receiving enough recent practice, exposure, or demonstrated competency in an expected training area.

Training gaps answer a Training Officer's question: **Based on what our people have actually been doing, what should we train on next?**

A training gap is not a generic incomplete record.

- An overdue assignment belongs in **Needs Action**.
- An expired or expiring certification belongs in **Credentials**.
- A missing required task-book item belongs in the member's assigned work/readiness record.
- A training gap identifies an opportunity for future training based on evidence across completed training and evaluations.

## Gap types

### Exposure gap

There is little or no recent recorded practice in an important topic.

Example: a shift has not practiced ground ladders in nine months.

### Frequency gap

Recorded training volume is below a department-defined target.

Example: driver training target is six hours per year and a member has two recorded hours.

### Competency gap

Recent evaluations show repeated difficulty or remediation follow-up in a skill area.

Example: several members required remediation during pump-operations evaluations.

### Experience gap — future signal

Quick Add or other experience records may eventually identify limited real-world exposure, such as driver hours or uncommon call types. Experience gaps must remain distinct from competency gaps. Limited exposure does not mean a member is incompetent.

## Interpretation and safety rules

Responder Roadmap must distinguish among:

1. **Lack of evidence** — insufficient records to determine recent practice.
2. **Lack of recent practice** — enough historical evidence exists, but the topic has not been practiced recently.
3. **Below training target** — recorded volume is below a department-defined expectation.
4. **Demonstrated performance concern** — evaluation evidence shows remediation or repeated difficulty.

The product must not label a member "not competent" solely because training hours, exposure, or records are missing.

## Dashboard behavior

The Training Officer dashboard should present this feature as **Training opportunities identified**, not as a generic count of missing records.

Each surfaced opportunity should explain:

- the training topic;
- the gap type;
- how many members are affected;
- the evidence that caused it to surface;
- the time window used;
- the recommended next action.

Examples:

- **Pump Operations — Exposure gap**: 6 members have no recent practice. Last department practice was 7 months ago. Recommended action: Schedule drill.
- **Ground Ladders — Frequency gap**: 4 members are below the annual training target. Recommended action: Assign training.
- **Patient Assessment — Competency gap**: 3 members have repeated remediation follow-up. Recommended action: Targeted skills practice.

The dashboard should prioritize the most meaningful opportunities and link to the full analysis rather than displaying every raw gap.

## Data sources

Current and future analysis may use:

- completed department training;
- training hours and department targets;
- task-book and assignment completion history;
- graded skill evaluations and remediation follow-up;
- class/training-sheet records;
- Quick Add practice/activity data when appropriate.

Incomplete or ambiguous evidence should be described as limited evidence, not converted into a competency judgment.

## Recommended actions

Gap signals should lead directly to workflow actions when possible:

- **Schedule drill**
- **Assign training**
- **Create class**
- **Review affected members**
- **Targeted skills practice**

## Reporting principle

Training gaps are a decision-support feature, not an official declaration of qualification or competency. Department policy, credential requirements, evaluator decisions, and official qualification records remain authoritative.
